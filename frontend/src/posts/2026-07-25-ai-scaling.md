title: AI Scaling
---

A thought about AI:

AI makes it easier to build and maintain more complex systems: for example at my job, my infrastructure tools team can more quickly build more tools, more technical resources, and much more supporting documentation: design docs, technical docs, decision docs, user docs. So long as users also can use AI, they can also navigate the complexity more easily. Systems are pressured to grow in size and complexity as their owners push to gain more predictability and control within their working environments.

So long as users have access to AI, they'll be fine and there are potential shared benefits from the bureaucracies and greater scale of information development and processing. But the systems will become less accessible to users without AI, especially if and when they have less ability to exert the pressure to make them accessible. Information systems become gatekept by the owners of AI and its required technical infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the costs of AI may go up: the popular theory of AI development is that LLMs become more intelligent as you pour more resources (data, compute) into them. Investment money will dry up (unless it somehow doesn't? Will growth feed on debt indefinitely?) and there will become increased pressure to turn profit, which will further increase costs to users. Efficiency gains exist and it's unpredictable how things will balance out. I posit that in order to get the levels of intelligence needed for the super-powered systems we're hoping for, plus the limits of the burning planet, efficiency gains will need to be massive and unprecedented.

The outcome, besides the burning planet, is the increasing imbalance of power between those who own the infrastructure and those who don't. Being able to pay for AI (rent the infrastructure) is bounded to the renters' actual possession of those funds. As AI becomes better at human labor than humans, the non-owners of AI lose the relative ability to generate new economic value. And wealth flows inexorably to the rentiers.

"AI is bad" is a popular view and this seems straightforward enough to me. Are there interesting related arguments that folks can point me to? And please share your own thoughts!
