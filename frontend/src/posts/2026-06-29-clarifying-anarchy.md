title: Clarifying Anarchy
---

As I think about political philosophy and society I often find that "anticapitalism" and "anarchism" are central words to describe my view and opinions. I notice that those words don't exactly mean to others what I want them to mean. I was wondering: what's a terse symbol that represents my view? Other people call themselves "socialist", "Marxist", "liberal"; a lot of people I talk to wouldn't claim an exact identifier, might say "generally left-leaning", maybe. I want a specific identifier, out of a desire to be understood. New idea: are there AUTHORS whose analysis best represent my view?

I decided to ask Claude.

I don't want to share with you what Claude said. I think that in general what an AI says unfiltered is not an interesting form of sharing. I think the experience and consequences of talking to AI are fascinating. But I tried to summarize my "view" within my prompt, and I decided that is itself worth sharing. I invite opinions. About political philosophy, about AI, about whatever.

Prompt: 
(unclarity and grammatical errors are handled easily by Claude, and that's partly why talking to it is fun.)

> Anticapitalism: I think it's a valuable concept but I realized I mean by it something different from what most leftists I hear from mean by it. I don't think "endless profits and growth" is per se a bad thing. I think that the arrangement that profits go to the owners of capital is a fundamental problem, that negotiation between power- imbalanced  parties serves the more powerful party. This is a component of exploitation. Shared profits is the better alternative. I think the many forms of alienation from capitalism are interesting and important, and I think that's a strength of Marxist critique. But I don't think that "landlords are bad" follows in the way that many leftists I talk to think so. I think there is value in a bureaucracy that reduces underhanded dealing and free riders is bad, and I don't think poor people on piddly benefits are the free riders, I think the wealthy who accumulate wealth without contributing to society are the free riders. I think institutionalized violence and credentialism as tools of oppression are a bad thing. I think that... competitive markets are a good thing. I think that centralized planning in excess is a bad thing. I think that an ideal society is one that balances collective planning and care, with individual responsibility and freedom, maximizes human flourishing. So which author or authors bring these together into a coherent analysis? When people ask what are my politics I think that "anarchy" and "anticapitalism" are my favorite words but they clearly miss the mark and are misinterpreted. I think a well-orgnaized state is necessary and will unavoidably always exist. I agree with the anarchist critique of socialism that the best forms of work are not electoral. So. What's my "ism"?

I was freshly thinking about this because of recent encounters with some Marxist ideas: in a reading group and in an activist tenants' organization that is expressly Maoist, and I am prompted to describe my view which I was only able to say, "well I kind of like Anarchism".

---

Ok I wrote that two days ago. On more reflection, I decided the concepts most central to my thinking on Anarchy, and the way it relates to my life, my activities, and my political views, are the particular goals of:
* Most definitionally: a minimizing of hierarchy and domination.
To get there, we maximize:
* Voluntary participation; a subcomponent of non-domination
* Movement toward shared values and goals, as opposed to negotiation in self-interest.
* Non-violence; which is another subcomponent of non-domination since domination is achieved through exercise of violence.
* Ongoing creative practice and contribution by all participants

This doesn't give me a "particular recipe for structuring a state"; for example there are limited situations where "consensus decision-making" in its purest form is feasible, and even where it's practiced it often falls back to alternatives. So I don't think my Anarchy qualifies as a political ideology. I'm also a democrat, in the sense that I believe "voting is a good basis for governance", to a first approximation.

I also think the existence of the state is necessary and inevitable and my favorite definition of the state is "the body that claims a monopoly on the legitimate use of violence, within a certain region or group". I.e., there's a general agreement that some people who commit certain crimes need to be managed in some way through compulsion, domination, and violence. And a project of "a good state" is to maximize that legitimacy.

I also like the concept that a society's goal should be "maximal human flourishing"; but that sounds like Utilitarianism and I'm pretty sure that school of thought diverges in practice from some of my preferences.

I didn't get my "terse symbol to represent my view" but those are overrated.
